Brinjal is typically cultivated in warm climates, requiring well-drained, loamy soils with optimal
pH levels ranging between 6.0 and 7.0 for better growth. The crop grows well under both
irrigated and rain-fed conditions, but its cultivation is often constrained by various biotic and
abiotic factors. Among the biotic stresses, pest infestations are a major limiting factor in brinjal
production, leading to substantial losses in yield and quality.
Over 36 insect species have been documented to attack brinjal at different stages of its growth.
Pests such as the jassid (Amrasca biguttula biguttula Ishida), aphid (Aphis persicae Sulzer),
whitefly (Bemisia tabaci Gennadius) and coccinellid beetle (Epilachna spp.) can cause
significant damage to the plant foliage, flowers, and fruits. Among these, the most notorious pest
is the brinjal shoot and fruit borer (BSFB), Leucinodes orbonalis (Lepidoptera: Pyralidae),

Future scope
Future research can focus on developing pest-resistant brinjal
varieties, integrating advanced monitoring technologies, and
enhancing biological control methods. Expanding farmer
education programs and policy support can boost IPM adoption
and reduce chemical pesticide reliance. Climate-resilient
strategies and environmental impact studies will ensure
sustainable and adaptable pest management practices.
